SapTap

What the mod adds

One new block, in thirteen metals: a sap tap — a real spile, with a tapered tip that bites into the bark, a collar that seats against the trunk, a tube with a hammered band and a drip lip, and a hook rod underneath.

The bucket is not part of the tap model. When you hang one, the block entity tessellates the actual vanilla woodbucket mesh and drops it onto the hook, so what hangs there is the same bucket you were holding — bail, staves, iron rings and all. It also means any modded bucket that follows the vanilla pattern hangs correctly.

Nothing in vanilla is overridden. Resin logs still harvest by hand exactly as before; the tap is an alternative that trades waiting for a much larger yield per tree.

Crafting a tap

Shapeless, in any crafting grid. The three items can sit in any slots, in any order:

Either stock works: the smithed metal plate item (metalplate-<metal>) or the metal sheet block (metalsheet-<metal>). They look almost identical in the inventory, so the recipe accepts both. Only the plate has a vanilla crafting route — sheets are loot and worldgen only — so the plate is the repeatable survival option.

Tool hardness

Working the stock needs a hammer and a chisel at least as hard as the metal being worked. The two tools need not match each other. Neither is consumed; each craft costs 8 durability off both.

Watch for

Copper hammers and chisels cannot make any tap at all — not even a copper one. Copper shares its tool tier with gold and silver, which is not hard enough to cut plate. Bronze is the earliest tooling that works.

Available metals

Thirteen, each taking its colour from that metal's ingot texture. The metal is cosmetic — a lead tap collects exactly as fast as a steel one.

Tapping a tree

  1. Find a log that is oozing resin.Vanilla pine and acacia grow them — the blocks whose code starts log-resin-. A plain log will not take a tap.
  2. Right-click the side of that log with a tap in hand.Only the four vertical faces; you cannot tap the top or bottom of a trunk. The tap intercepts the click, so the log's usual hand-harvest does not fire instead.
  3. Right-click the tap with an empty wooden bucket.It hangs from the hook and collection starts from that moment. The bucket must be genuinely empty.
  4. Wait.Looking at the tap shows the litres collected so far and the season's current yield multiplier.
  5. Right-click with an empty hand to take the bucket back.The bucket returns to your hand and the sap comes with it as resin items — one resin per full litre.

The two-tap limit

A tree accepts at most two taps. Connected trunk blocks are found by walking outward through the tree-felling group the game already assigns to grown trees, so the limit is genuinely per tree rather than per resin patch — moving up the same trunk to a different resin block will not get you a third. Player-placed logs carry no felling group, so those fall back to matching wood type.

How fast it fills

The base rate is 0.5 litres per in-game hour into a 10-litre bucket, multiplied by the season the game calendar reports at the tap's own position — so it is hemisphere-aware, and a tap in the southern hemisphere runs on the opposite schedule to one in the north.

Sap accrues against the world calendar, not against real time, and the tap keeps its last-updated timestamp when the chunk unloads. Leave the area, come back a week later, and the elapsed hours are credited on the next tick — you do not have to stand there.

Note

That catch-up is applied at whatever the rate is when the chunk loads again. A bucket left through the winter and collected on the first day of spring is credited at the spring rate for the whole stretch — and the reverse is true too, so collecting the moment a good season ends is worth doing.

Collecting

Right-click the tap with an empty hand. The bucket goes back to your hand first, then the resin is handed over — one item per full litre. If your inventory is full, the resin drops at the tap rather than vanishing.

Take a bucket early and the game tells you what you got: "The bucket is not full yet: 6.4 / 10 litres." A full one reports "Collected 10 resin from the sap tap."

Watch for

Partial litres are lost. Taking the bucket down pays out only whole litres and resets the tap to zero — at 6.4 litres you get 6 resin and the remaining 0.4 is gone. There is no way to peek and re-hang without paying that cost, so let it finish where you can.

Breaking a tap that has a bucket on it is safe: both the bucket and the sap collected so far drop as items. The tap itself always drops as the plain north-facing variant, so a broken tap can be re-placed on any face.

Reference

Block code. side names the direction from the tap towards the tree, and rotates the shape; all thirteen metals share one handbook entry.

What a tap pays out. One per full litre of sap.

Any block whose code contains bucket and which declares liquidContainerProps. Modded buckets that follow the vanilla pattern work without a patch.
Creative & handbook
